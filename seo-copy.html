The gap

You'll live to 79.
You'll stop living at 66.

That difference has a name — the healthspan gap, and in the United States it averages 12.4 years. It is the stretch at the end where you are technically alive and functionally finished. Nobody plans for it. Everybody walks into it. And it does not arrive as a diagnosis; it arrives as a series of small surrenders you barely notice giving up.

Untrained trajectory Trained trajectory

The upper curve is not genetics. It is not luck. It is the accumulated result of a few thousand small decisions about load, movement, protein, and sleep — decisions that compound in exactly one direction for whoever makes them consistently.

You cannot borrow strength at 75. There is no catch-up window. Every year you spend untrained is a withdrawal from an account you will desperately need to draw on later. Starfighter Fitness exists to make the deposits — deliberately, on a schedule, with someone watching.

Surrender 01

You start taking the handrail.

Not because you need it. Because one day you almost did, and now you always will. That's the first thing you hand over, and you never take it back.

Surrender 02

Your grandkid asks you to get on the floor. You say no.

You say it lightly. You make a joke out of it. But you both know what you actually said, and the next generation just learned what old is supposed to look like.

Surrender 03

Somebody starts carrying things for you.

The suitcase. The groceries. The bag of soil in the parking lot. It's kindness, and it's a diagnosis, and it arrives roughly a decade before anyone says it out loud.

Surrender 04

The trip gets planned without you.

Everyone's very nice about it. There's a lot of walking, they say. Too much standing. And that is the year your world quietly shrinks to the size of your living room.

Straight answers

Questions people
actually ask.

I'm 58 and I haven't trained in a decade. Am I too late?

No. Late is the most common starting condition we see, and the gains available in the first ninety days to someone starting from zero are larger than for almost anyone else. What we do adjust is the on-ramp — Phase 01 exists precisely so that technique and consistency get built before load does.

Do I need a gym?

No. Three of the five tracks are built for training at home — two on bands and dumbbells, one on dumbbells alone. You'll be placed on the track that matches what you actually own and the number of days you can honestly commit to.

What happens in the first week?

You'll complete a PAR-Q and health history, sign an image release, and log three days of food exactly as you normally eat — no adjusting for the audience. Then you'll have your Initial Strategy Call, get placed on a track, and your 90-day plan gets built around what came out of that conversation. On Precision, your genetic panel is completed in that same window and the plan is built off the results.

Who actually coaches me?

A Starfighter coach, matched to your schedule, your goals, and the equipment you actually have. Every coach works from the same programming library, is trained on the same system, and is held to the same response times — so the standard doesn't change based on who picks up your file.

What does the genetic panel actually tell you?

It gives your coach a real starting point instead of a template — how you tend to respond to training volume, how quickly you recover between hard sessions, and how you process what you eat. It informs your programming, your nutrition targets, and your recovery structure from day one rather than after eight weeks of trial and error. The results belong to you.

Who prescribes the peptides?

An independent licensed pharmacist we partner with — not us. They review your health history and your panel and make every prescribing decision themselves, at their discretion and under their license. Eligibility depends on your history and your state's law, and it is never guaranteed. Starfighter Fitness neither prescribes nor dispenses anything.

Is the coaching itself medical care?

No. The coaching side of Starfighter Fitness is nonclinical fitness and nutrition coaching — we don't diagnose, treat, or prescribe, and we don't replace your physician. Anything clinical runs through the licensed pharmacist or your own doctor, and we'll tell you plainly when something belongs there.

What happens at day 90?

A reassessment against the baseline you set on day one, and a real conversation: renew at the standard rate and run another block, move to a maintenance option, or take what you've built and go. There's no auto-enrollment into a fourth month you didn't choose.

I don't live near you. Does that matter?

Not at all. Coaching is delivered remotely nationwide — programming and check-ins through the app, calls over Zoom. Clients in all 50 states are on the same system.
